focusing system. 2 
The mirrors are dielectric coated, with Ravg ≥98% @ 405 -  700 nm at angle of incidence 3 
from 30 to 60 degrees. The size of the mirror is 30 mm × 35 mm. The linearity of motors is 4 
99.9%. The long-term drift of motors over 8 hours: < 0.5 mRad. The machinery scan angle of 5 
XY scanning is ±  11°. The control voltage range is ±  5 V. The repeatability, indicated by 6 
relative root mean square error (RRMSE), are 0.058, 0.064, and 0.033 for x, y, and z, 7 
respectively (Figure S2 and S3). 8

Supplementary Note 2.  1 
Minimize aberration with scanning tube lens 2 
The excitation field distortion is a common problem for 3D dynamic focusing system 1. In 3 
piezoelectric stage-based imaging system, the sample is moved step by step. Therefore, in 4 
this situation the excitation and detection path remain stationary, which avoided the 5 
aberration and field distortion. However, in a 3D dynamic focusing system, the galvanometer 6 
mirror is tilted to steer the beam,  and the light will be deflected off its initial straight (optical 7 
axis) path, which could induce severe aberration. Instead of using standard spherical or 8 
achromatic doublet lenses for relaying the beam after the 3D dynamic focusing system, we 9 
employed a laser scanning tube lens. This laser scanning tube lens can create a telecentric 10 
system, providing flat field and aberration reduced focused laser after objective lens1.  11 
To demonstrate the advantage of using a laser scanning tube lens over conventional 12 
achromatic doublet lenses in 3D -DyFI, we conduct 3D imaging of 200 nm red fluorescent 13 
microspheres under both conditions (Fig. S1 3). In 3D -DyFI with achromatic doublet lenses, 14 
the image of fluorescent microspheres exhibits severe distortions and spherical divergence, 15 
especially in the area further away from the center of the image (Fig. S1 3a). In contrast, the 16 
laser scanning tube lens delivers uniformly clear images across the entire imaging field ( Fig. 17 
S13b), showing pronounced aberration- correcting capabilities. In this microsphere sample, 18 
microspheres are located on the surface of the coverslip. Theoretically, these microspheres 19 
should have the same axial position, as shown in the image taken with laser scanning tube 20 
lens base 3D-DyFI (Fig. S13b, maximum projected x-z and y-z images). However, the image 21 
taken with the achromatic doublet lens base 3D -DyFI shows obvious axial position variation 22 
(Fig. S13a, maximum projected x- z and y -z images). These results further validate the 23 
correction capacity of the laser scanning tube lens in 3D -DyFI, which ensures a flat and 24 
uniform field scanning. Additionally, the use of laser scanning tube lenses significantly 25 
enhances the axial resolution of 3D -DyFI. As shown in Fig. S13, it is evident that the using 26 
of conventional achromatic doublet lenses produced an axial PSF elongation by 27 
approximately 2 times compared with that use scanning tube lens. Altogether, the using 28 
scanning tube lenses in 3D -DyFI provides distortion and aberration reduced excitation, 29 

17 
 
ensuring accurate and high- resolution 3D imaging. The aberration can be further reduced by 1 
using adaptive optics or spatial light modulators, at the cost of system complexity 2-4. Another 2 
way to reduce the aberration is use of long working distance objective lenses 5. 3
