Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
Li et al. demonstrates three-dimensional (3D) random-access confocal fluorescence imaging using a commercially available
3D dynamic focusing system (FL8220-3D-500, Sino-Galvo). Compared to a conventional 3D piezoelectric stage, the device
enables high-speed imaging up with a refresh rate of up to 500 Hz for imaging three distant fluorescent beads, which is 25
times faster than a piezostage-based scanning method. Several specific aspects in the manuscript could be addressed to
potentially clarify the significance of the work. 
Technical aspects: 
[1] In section “Configuration and 3D Resolution characterization of 3D-DyFI,” the optical principle of remote focusing is
unclear. As a reader in optics field, I’d like to understand the details of the configuration of the plano-convex lens and two
lenses in the remote focusing system. This includes: (1) the focal lengths and distances of each lens, and (2) specifications
of the used galvo mirrors and linear actuators. The essential principles of remote focusing should be more elaborated in the
main text, and the details about the dynamic focusing system should be clarified at least in the supplementary section. 
[2] The reported FWHM shows poorer resolution in 3D-DyFI, but this is not clearly indicated in the manuscript. I strongly
suspect that the intrinsic spherical aberrations due to the array of plano-concave lens and two convex lenses caused the
resolution degradation. Such indications and attributions of poorer 3D-DyFI should be fairly mentioned in the revised
manuscript. 
[3] In Fig. 2, I am also interested in the bidirectional repeatability of the system. If possible, could you provide data on
whether the galvo mirrors and the z-axis linear actuators exhibited any mechanical hysteresis? If so, how much bidirectional
repeatability is guaranteed? 
Contents aspects: 
[4] Would you kindly consider merging the contents of Fig. 6 and 7? I believe that the authors can clearly compare the
performance gap between 3D-DyFI and the conventional piezostage-based scanning method in a cleverer way. As a reader,
I had difficulty comparing the performance between them in the current version of the manuscript. 
In the introduction and discussion, some relevant and essential references are missing: 
[5] For instance, the manuscript should explicitly clarify that the concept of the remote focusing is not completely new. For
this, please add the following references: (i) Opt. Express 16, 21843-21848 (2008) (ii) Proceedings of the National Academy
of Sciences 108.49 (2011): 19504-19509, (iii) Optics Express 29.5 (2021): 6293-6301. Please then compare how the
proposed method in the manuscript is different from conventional methods and what advantages it has.

index gradient lens, which can also offer high-speed lateral random-access imaging and axial scanning, respectively.
Please cite the following references: (i) eLife 11:e73266 (2022), (2) Optics letters 33.18 (2008): 2146-2148. What is the
advantage of the proposed method compared to using such devices? 
Reviewer #2 

(Remarks to the Author) 
In this manuscript, the authors propose a 3D random-access confocal microscopy with remote-focusing system, namely 3D-
DyFI, as a technique able to perform 3D raster scanning imaging and 3D random-access imaging. To validate the
performance of the proposed 3D-DyFI, the authors perform 3D imaging of fluorescent beads and Hela cells by considering
the 3D piezo stage-based confocal microscopy as ground truth. In this way, they demonstrate that their solution is simple,
cost-effective, and fast with respect to a standard technology. In particular, the authors demonstrate that their system has an
isotropic response time across the x, y, and z axes, with a 34-fold improvement in axial response time over traditional piezo
stages, and with the possibility to continuously monitoring the signals in three different planes, showing a refresh rate of 500
Hz on 12 two different positions in 3D. 
The topic of this manuscript could be of interest for the readers of the Communications Engineering journal. In general, the
manuscript is well written and easy to read and understand even by a reader who is not expert in this research topic. The
authors present a complete and precise description of the methods, with all details needed to reproduce them. Most of the
claims are well supported by the exhaustive validation proposed in the Results section. Therefore, in my opinion, this
manuscript has the potential to be accepted in this journal, but the following major revisions must first be addressed. 
1. In the abstract, the authors say that “this method is also compatible with multiphoton imaging and multicolor imaging in
principle.” However, in the manuscript they don’t demonstrate this. Thus, this claim must be removed from the abstract and
must be reported only in the Discussion section. 
2. In the first sub-section of the Results, the authors measure the resolution of their system. It results that the resolution of the
proposed 3D-DyFI system (344 nm × 518 nm × 1.02 μm) is worse than the piezoelectric stage (273 nm × 451 nm × 0.9 μm).
However, throughout the manuscript, the authors only claim that the 3D-DyFI system is simple, cost-effective, and fast with
respect to the piezoelectric stage. In my opinion, to be clear to the readers, the authors must add this aspect about resolution
when they discuss the advantages of their system. 
3. In the first sub-section of the Results, the authors say that “It is worth to note that due to the relatively large size of the
fluorescent beads (200 nm), the measured FWHM value could be larger than the actual resolution of 3D-DyFI.” This means
that the reported resolution (344 nm × 518 nm × 1.02 μm) is not the real one. In my opinion, it is needed to measure and
report the real resolution of the system. 
4. The resolution of the 3D-DyFI system is not isotropic in the three directions of the 3D space (344 nm × 518 nm × 1.02 μm),
as well as the resolution of the piezoelectric stage (273 nm × 451 nm × 0.9 μm). Do the authors have any idea to make this
resolution more isotropic? This could be an additional advantage compared to the piezoelectric stage.

directed the laser to  a target plate over a long distance. By varying the voltage applied to the 
dynamic focusing system in the x and y directions and monitoring the resultant spot motion, we 
determined that the minimum responsive voltage is 0.0705 mV , which corresponds to a 
displacement of 20 nm at the imaging focal plane.  It should be noted that a 20 dB attenuator is 
positioned between the output of the FPGA data acquisition card and the input to the dynamic  
focusing system. The resolution of data acquisition card (PCIe-7858, NI) analog output is 16  bit 
with a ± 10 V output range. Consequently, its analog voltage output resolution is 0.3 mV , which 
can be enhanced to 0.003 mV using a 20 dB attenuator. 
No attenuator was used for z-axis control. While the responsive voltage can reach 0.0705 
mV , the resolution is constrained by the analog voltage output resolution of the FPGA data 
acquisition card, which is 0.3 mV . Referring to Figure 2, the minimum sampling distance in the 
z-axis is determined to be 0.29 nm. 
In summary, the minimum sampling distance is 20 nm in the x and y directions and 0.29 
nm in the z direction.
